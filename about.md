I'm Óscar Chávez Ortiz. I recently completed my Ph.D. in Astronomy at The University of Texas at Austin, where I studied how Lyman-alpha light escapes from galaxies and what that reveals about the reionization of the early universe. Along the way, I built a parallel set of skills in data pipelines, machine learning, and statistical inference — tools I now bring to a different kind of problem.

<!-- PLACEHOLDER: confirm exact current title and institution/employer -->
I currently work in higher education, in assessment and data analytics, where I apply the same rigor I developed in astrophysics research — careful measurement, uncertainty quantification, and reproducible analysis — to institutional data and decision-making.

What motivates me hasn't changed much since graduate school: I like taking a messy, large dataset and finding the signal in it, whether that signal is a galaxy's chemical history or a pattern in how students learn. I'm equally interested in the people side of that work — mentoring students, building tools other people can use, and making technical work legible to the people who need to act on it.

## Outreach & Mentorship {#outreach-mentorship}

A core part of my work as an astronomer has been sharing science beyond the classroom and research group. I'm deeply committed to outreach, mentorship, and creating pathways into STEM for students from historically underrepresented backgrounds.

### Astronomy outreach

I regularly participate in outreach activities aimed at making astronomy more accessible to undergraduates and the general public — focusing on making astrophysical concepts intuitive, interactive, and exciting.

- Public talks on galaxies and cosmic evolution — including an [Astronomy on Tap talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XBiJdVuamTg) at Celis Brewery in the summer of 2024, on why Lyman-alpha matters.
- Hands-on demonstrations explaining spectroscopy, redshift, and how astronomers study distant galaxies.
- Departmental outreach events connecting students with active researchers.

### Mentorship &amp; student support

Mentorship has been a defining part of my academic journey, and I try to offer students navigating STEM pathways the same support I received.

- Mentor undergraduate and graduate students interested in astronomy and data science, through programs including GUMMY and [GEVIP](https://sites.utexas.edu/vip/).
- Support first-generation and undocumented students pursuing higher education — see my [AstroBites article](https://astrobites.org/2024/07/24/undocumented_phd/) on this.
- Advise students on graduate school applications, research opportunities, and fellowships.
- Served as a graduate student representative, advocating for communication and student support within the UT Astronomy program.
- Co-created **HackAstro-Academy**, a Python course at UT modeled on UC Berkeley's Python for Astronomers — [see it on GitHub](https://github.com/ochase10/HackAstro-Academy).

### Community &amp; departmental engagement

- Helped organize departmental social and professional-development events, through UGSPS and [Cafecito Cósmico](https://sites.utexas.edu/cafecitocosmico/).
- Contributed to initiatives that foster collaboration and inclusivity.
- Helped design outreach programs connecting research with public audiences.

I'm always happy to collaborate with schools, student organizations, and community groups interested in astronomy, higher-education pathways, or careers in STEM — [reach out](/contact/) any time.
