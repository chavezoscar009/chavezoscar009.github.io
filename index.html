Óscar A. Chávez Ortiz

I recently completed my Ph.D. in Astronomy at The University of Texas at Austin,
where my research focused on how Lyman-alpha light escapes from galaxies and what
that reveals about reionization in the early universe. I now work as an Assessment
Analyst for the Career Services department at San Diego State University, bringing
the same data-driven rigor from my research to questions about student outcomes
and career readiness. I leverage big-data analytics, automated pipelines, and ML
frameworks to uncover the complex mapping between data and the questions that matter.

Research Focus

Understanding Lyman-alpha escape from galaxies to improve reionization predictions

I design and implement scalable data pipelines to process large observational datasets (≥1M records), engineer predictive features, and train machine learning models to infer key galaxy properties. My work emphasizes reproducibility, uncertainty quantification, and robust model evaluation, integrating Bayesian inference and probabilistic modeling to extract insights from diverse astronomical data.
